| D-14 | Thresholds for "needs food" and "needs water" (hours). Food: 24 h since the last counted feeding log if it was a full bowl, 12 h if partial (accepted, see D-16). Highlight levels: three colours per type, each with an icon or label as well as the colour (accepted): green inside the window, orange from 75% of it (18 h for a full bowl or water, 9 h for partial), red once it expires; the early morning and late evening routine is guidance text only (accepted). Water: 24 h since the last counted water log, flat, no amount level (accepted). A type with no counted log (unverified logs do not count) is red once the spot has any data (a counted log of either type, or a recorded cat: approved cats only, R-04, from Phase 5) and grey with a "no data" icon only when the spot has no data at all (accepted). | D-18 | Feeding and water logs need no photo at the start. A log counts when the phone is within 50 m of the spot (the proof radius, a constant) and R-10 and R-13 pass. At most 30 counted logs per member per rolling 24 hours (R-13). The phone's own coordinates are not stored. | accepted |
| D-19 | "This spot was empty" button, for food or for water: makes that type red until the next counted log, marks the latest counted log of that type disputed, and does not penalise the logger by itself (an empty bowl usually means the cats ate). The reporter must be within the proof radius, and a report never disputes the reporter's own log (R-16). | accepted |
| D-21 | Profile picture: the member's Google account photo. Google's `picture` claim arrives through Cognito's attribute mapping at each sign-in; our server alone copies it into `avatar_url`, only if it is a Google image address, and members cannot write `avatar_url`. We keep only the URL and never copy the image into our storage. The picture is shown only if the member chose to show it (D-27); otherwise, and when Google sends none (the claim is not guaranteed), a default avatar with the member's initials is shown. No upload and no admin review in v1: the member opts in to showing a photo from their own Google account, which Google moderates, so the hard rules stay unchanged. Changing the picture inside the app is v2, with a limit and moderation. | accepted |
| D-22 | Money guardrails: the app stays free for the first 6 months on the AWS Free plan credit ($100 at sign-up plus up to $100 for activities). The plan ends after 6 months or when the credit is used, then the account closes unless upgraded to Paid (AWS keeps the data 90 days). A daily moderation cap of about $0.50 (R-05; at most $15 a month, about $91 over the 6 free months), an AWS Budget of $20 a month with email alerts, and an upgrade to the Paid plan before the plan ends. | accepted |
| D-23 | When a limit is hit (daily cap R-05, repeat log R-10, 30 logs R-13, 60 attempts R-17) the request is refused with an error and nothing is saved. Only a failed location check saves a log, as unverified (R-09). | accepted |
| D-25 | The phone must report a location accuracy of 50 m or better for a log to count, so a vague reading cannot pass the proof radius check (R-09). The limit is a constant. | accepted |
| D-26 | DynamoDB capacity mode (Phase 1): the always-free allowance (25 GB, 25 write and 25 read units per Region and account) is for provisioned capacity only; on-demand tables are billed (Tel Aviv: $0.85 per million writes, $0.17 per million reads, under $1 a month at 1,000 members). Decision: provisioned, splitting the 25 units across the tables, and on-demand only for a table that outgrows its share. | accepted |
| D-27 | The app asks three things at the first sign-in: the member's full name, whether to show their Google photo, and their city. The map opens centred on that city. | accepted |
| D-28 | Details of D-27: asked once, editable later in the profile. Full name and city are required free text, checked like all text (R-04). The city is turned into a map centre by a lookup service (D-29). The photo question has no pre-selected answer. With "no", or without a Google photo, the initials avatar is shown. | accepted |
| D-29 | City lookup service, to centre the map (D-27). Options: Nominatim, the OSM public server (free; allowed for apps at moderate traffic, at most 1 request a second, identifying User-Agent, results cached, no autocomplete, can be withdrawn), or Amazon Location Service (20,000 free lookups a month for only 3 months, then paid, and it counts against the daily cap R-05; see [AWS.md](AWS.md#costs)). Decision: Nominatim, called from the server once per new city and cached in DynamoDB; switch to Amazon Location Service if it is blocked. | accepted |
| D-30 | Responsive: phone, tablet, desktop, mobile first. The floor is 360 px wide (common Android phones), which also covers the iPhone XS (375 x 812 px), the smallest iPhone tested (R-18). Breakpoints 768 px (tablet) and 1024 px (desktop) live in one CSS file. | accepted (floor); breakpoints assumed |
| D-31 | Permissions are asked on the first load, not when first needed: a short explanation screen, then one tap starts the browser prompt. Only location is asked, the one permission v1 needs: it is what the proof radius checks for logs and empty reports read (R-09, R-16, precise reading per D-25). No camera prompt: logs have no photo (D-18), and photos for spots and cats come from the phone's own picker, which shows no browser prompt. Refusal never blocks the app (an unlocated log is unverified, R-09); the profile says how to turn it back on. The screen shows once per device, and is skipped when the Permissions API says location is already granted. A browser may ask again or block repeated prompts, so nothing depends on the grant. | accepted (ask at first load); details proposed |
| D-32 | Strings: `he.json` and `en.json`, same keys, namespaced by feature (`map.title`), on the CDN under `/locales/`, loaded at runtime (react-i18next + i18next-http-backend), not bundled, so wording changes without an app release. Key types come from `en.json` (type import only). Plural keys are compared per language (Hebrew has a "two" form). If a file cannot load and none is cached, show an icon-only retry screen, since no string may be hardcoded. A new language is one JSON file plus one entry (code, direction, label) in `src/constants/`. | accepted (two files, CDN); loading details proposed |
| D-33 | Code conventions: no inline styles (no `style` attribute, no `element.style`); styles live in a `.css` file beside the component (CSS Modules), varying by class or `data-*` (e.g. the marker levels of D-14). No magic numbers in logic: limits, timeouts, sizes, counts, radii go in `src/constants/`; visual values (spacing, colours, breakpoints) in `src/styles/`. Both are lint errors, not guidance. | accepted; CSS Modules assumed |
| D-34 | Municipal stations: tiered pipeline (ArcGIS API, published lists, manual) run by a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow that opens a pull request; merge deploys one GeoJSON file to the CDN; the map reads it as a read-only layer from Phase 2 ([SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)). Address-only lists are geocoded with Nominatim, as in D-29 (a recurring script may send at most 4 requests a minute, results cached in git), and a person checks every point in the pull request. Tested on the 25 Tel Aviv stations against the city's own coordinates: 20 answered, 19 of those within 150 m (median 23 m), 5 found nothing (garden and car park names). | accepted (fetch, GeoJSON on CDN); pipeline and geocoder proposed |
| D-35 | Municipal stations are public: exact coordinates are shown, since the municipality already publishes them, so R-03 would get an exception for them. They are grey until a counted log exists (D-14). From Phase 4 members can log at them: import into `spots` (upsert by `source` and `source_id`) and stop loading the file in the client; the file stays published. A log counts only within the 50 m proof radius (D-18) and a geocoded point can be 100 m or more off, so a geocoded station is checked by a person before it becomes loggable. | proposed |
| D-36 | No domain at the start. The app, `/locales/` and `/data/` share one CloudFront distribution on its default `*.cloudfront.net` address (same origin, no CORS). Google sign-in uses the Cognito default domain `amazoncognito.com` (AWS docs); with only the basic sign-in scopes no app verification is needed. Not Netlify: its free `*.netlify.app` adds a second host, CORS and a second pipeline, and the free plan has a hard monthly credit cap that pauses sites (third-party reports: 300 credits, about 15 GB). Route 53 gives no free domain: registration is paid and a hosted zone is $0.50 a month (D-15: re-check prices). Buy one before public launch: AdSense reportedly refuses free subdomains, and moving origin later loses installs and sessions. The origin is one constant. | proposed |
| D-37 | Extensibility without speculation: boundaries are enforced by `fallow` (features never import each other, only through `index.ts`); what will grow is data, not code (languages, municipal sources, thresholds, breakpoints); an abstraction needs a second use; every convention gets a lint rule, test or hook ([extensibility.md](../.claude/instructions/extensibility.md)). | proposed |
